# Pre-Calculus

**Proposed course slug:** `precalculus` (unchanged)

## Purpose

With algebra fluency and the advanced-foundations bridge both in place,
Pre-Calculus can focus on what's genuinely specific to it: trigonometry,
from ratios through identities and graphing, plus the analytic geometry
(conics, an introduction to vectors and polar coordinates) that Calculus
III later builds into full 3D and vector calculus.

## Why it matters for calculus

Trig functions and their derivatives/integrals are a large fraction of
Calculus I and II's actual problem content. Radian measure specifically
is non-negotiable — every calculus formula involving sin/cos/tan assumes
radians, not degrees. Vectors previewed here become 3D vectors in
Calculus III; polar coordinates previewed here get full calculus treatment
in Calculus II.

## Prerequisites

Algebra I & II (Calculus-Focused), General Advanced Mathematical Foundations

## Units

### Unit 1: Trigonometric Ratios and Right Triangles
`unitSlug: trig-ratios-and-right-triangles` (existing slug)

**Why it matters for calculus:** Right-triangle trig is the geometric
intuition every trig identity and every related-rates problem involving
angles eventually reduces back to.

**Learning objectives:**
- Define sine, cosine, and tangent as ratios of a right triangle's sides
- Solve for missing sides or angles in a right triangle
- Use trig ratios to solve real-world right-triangle word problems

**Lessons:**
1. *SOH-CAH-TOA: Defining the Trig Ratios* (20 min)
2. *Solving Right Triangles* — finding missing sides and angles. (25 min)
3. *Right-Triangle Word Problems* — angles of elevation/depression. (20 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 7 (Trigonometric Functions, right-triangle sections); Khan Academy "Right triangles and trigonometry."

**Suggested topic tags:** `trigonometricRatios` (existing).

### Unit 2: The Unit Circle and Radian Measure
`unitSlug: unit-circle-and-radians` (existing slug)

**Why it matters for calculus:** Radians are the required unit for every
calculus formula involving trig functions. The unit circle is the mental
model behind evaluating trig functions at any angle, not just acute ones.

**Learning objectives:**
- Convert fluently between degrees and radians
- Locate and use key angles on the unit circle (multiples of 30° and 45°)
- Evaluate sine, cosine, and tangent at any angle on the unit circle,
  including negative angles and angles beyond 360°

**Lessons:**
1. *Radian Measure and Degree-Radian Conversion* (20 min)
2. *The Unit Circle* — building it, not just memorizing it. (30 min)
3. *Evaluating Trig Functions Using the Unit Circle* — reference angles, all four quadrants. (25 min)
4. *Coterminal and Negative Angles* (15 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 7 (Unit Circle sections); Khan Academy "Trigonometry: Unit circle"; Paul's Online Math Notes, Algebra/Trig review — "Unit Circle" page.

**Suggested topic tags:** `unitCircleAndRadians` (existing).

### Unit 3: Graphing Trigonometric Functions
`unitSlug: graphing-trig-functions` (existing slug)

**Why it matters for calculus:** Reading amplitude, period, and phase
shift off a graph is exactly the skill needed to sanity-check a
derivative or integral of a trig function against its graph.

**Learning objectives:**
- Graph sine and cosine functions, identifying amplitude, period, and midline
- Graph transformations of sine/cosine (vertical shift, phase shift)
- Graph tangent, cotangent, secant, and cosecant

**Lessons:**
1. *Graphing Sine and Cosine* — amplitude, period, midline. (25 min)
2. *Phase Shift and Vertical Shift* (20 min)
3. *Graphing Tangent, Cotangent, Secant, and Cosecant* (25 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 7 (Graphs of trig functions); Khan Academy "Trigonometric functions" (graphing sections).

**Suggested topic tags:** `graphingTrigFunctions` (existing).

### Unit 4: Trigonometric Identities
`unitSlug: trig-identities` (existing slug)

**Why it matters for calculus:** Simplifying with identities is a core
technique in Calculus II's trigonometric integration unit — most trig
integrals are unsolvable until rewritten with an identity first.

**Learning objectives:**
- State and apply the Pythagorean identities
- Apply sum, difference, double-angle, and half-angle identities
- Simplify a trig expression and verify a trig identity

**Lessons:**
1. *Pythagorean Identities* (20 min)
2. *Sum and Difference Identities* (25 min)
3. *Double-Angle and Half-Angle Identities* (25 min)
4. *Verifying Identities* — the "prove the left side equals the right side" skill. (25 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 8 (Trigonometric Identities and Equations); Paul's Online Math Notes, Algebra/Trig review — "Trig Formulas."

**Suggested topic tags:** `trigonometricIdentities` (existing).

### Unit 5: Inverse Trigonometric Functions
`unitSlug: inverse-trig-functions`

**Why it matters for calculus:** Inverse trig functions have their own
standard derivatives (Calculus I) and appear as the answer form for a
family of Calculus II integration problems (especially after trig
substitution).

**Learning objectives:**
- Evaluate inverse sine, cosine, and tangent, including their restricted domains
- Explain why the domain restriction is necessary
- Simplify compositions of trig and inverse trig functions

**Lessons:**
1. *Introduction to Inverse Trig Functions* — arcsin, arccos, arctan, and why domains are restricted. (25 min)
2. *Evaluating Inverse Trig Expressions* (20 min)
3. *Compositions of Trig and Inverse Trig Functions* — e.g., sin(arccos(x)). (25 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 7 (Inverse Trigonometric Functions); Khan Academy "Inverse trigonometric functions."

**Suggested topic tags:** propose new: `inverseTrigFunctions`.

### Unit 6: Solving Trigonometric Equations
`unitSlug: solving-trig-equations`

**Why it matters for calculus:** Finding where a trig function equals a
specific value (or where its derivative is zero) is a recurring step in
optimization and curve-analysis problems in Calculus I.

**Learning objectives:**
- Solve trigonometric equations over a restricted domain and over all real numbers
- Use identities to simplify an equation before solving
- Account for all solutions using the periodicity of trig functions

**Lessons:**
1. *Solving Basic Trig Equations* (25 min)
2. *Solving Trig Equations Using Identities First* (25 min)
3. *Finding All Solutions* — using periods to write the general solution. (20 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 8 (Solving Trigonometric Equations); Khan Academy "Trigonometric equations and identities."

**Suggested topic tags:** propose new: `solvingTrigEquations`.

### Unit 7: Introduction to Vectors (Two Dimensions)
`unitSlug: intro-to-vectors-2d`

**Why it matters for calculus:** This is the direct, deliberate preview of
Calculus III's vectors unit — same operations, one dimension lower, so
the jump to 3D later is a dimension change, not a brand-new idea.

**Learning objectives:**
- Represent a vector in component form and geometrically
- Add, subtract, and scale vectors
- Find a vector's magnitude and direction
- Compute the dot product of two vectors and interpret it

**Lessons:**
1. *Introduction to Vectors* — component form, magnitude, direction. (25 min)
2. *Vector Addition, Subtraction, and Scalar Multiplication* (20 min)
3. *The Dot Product* — computing it and interpreting it (including the angle-between-vectors formula). (25 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 8 (Vectors); Khan Academy "Vectors" (Precalculus unit, not the physics one).

**Suggested topic tags:** propose new: `introToVectors2D`.

### Unit 8: Conic Sections
`unitSlug: conic-sections` (existing slug)

**Why it matters for calculus:** Conics reappear as level curves and
quadric surfaces in Calculus III's multivariable unit; recognizing a
conic's standard form is also a recurring integration-setup skill in
Calculus II.

**Learning objectives:**
- Identify and graph circles, parabolas, ellipses, and hyperbolas from their standard-form equations
- Convert a conic's general-form equation to standard form by completing the square
- Identify a conic's key features (center, foci, vertices, asymptotes as applicable)

**Lessons:**
1. *Parabolas* — standard form, vertex, focus, directrix. (25 min)
2. *Circles and Ellipses* — standard form and graphing. (25 min)
3. *Hyperbolas* — standard form, asymptotes, graphing. (25 min)
4. *Converting General Form to Standard Form* — completing the square with two variables. (20 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 10 (Analytic Geometry); Khan Academy "Conic sections."

**Suggested topic tags:** `conicSections` (existing).

### Unit 9: Polar Coordinates (Introduction)
`unitSlug: intro-to-polar-coordinates`

**Why it matters for calculus:** A light introduction only — full polar
calculus (arc length, area, derivatives in polar form) is Calculus II's
job. This unit's only goal is fluency converting and graphing, so
Calculus II can start directly with calculus on curves it can already plot.

**Learning objectives:**
- Convert points and simple equations between polar and rectangular (Cartesian) coordinates
- Plot points given in polar coordinates
- Graph basic polar curves (circles, roses, cardioids) by recognizing their standard forms

**Lessons:**
1. *The Polar Coordinate System* — plotting points, converting to/from rectangular. (25 min)
2. *Graphing Basic Polar Curves* — circles, roses, cardioids by pattern recognition. (25 min)

**Free resources:** OpenStax *Precalculus 2e*, ch. 8 (Polar Coordinates); Khan Academy has limited polar coverage — supplement with Paul's Online Math Notes, Calculus II — "Polar Coordinates" (its opening, pre-calculus-level section only).

**Suggested topic tags:** propose new: `introToPolarCoordinates`.

## Suggested assessment structure

- **Unit quizzes** (5-8 questions each).
- **Course-end exam** (25-30 questions): weight heavily toward Units 2-4
  (unit circle, graphing, identities) since fluency there is assumed
  without re-teaching throughout Calculus I and II; include at least one
  vector and one polar question as a readiness check for Calculus III/II
  respectively.
